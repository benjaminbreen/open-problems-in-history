Two clues from Vienna

The clues point to Frederick Dent Grant, President Ulysses S. Grant’s eldest son and the U.S. minister in Vienna. His wife was named Ida, a name audible near 1:01. A few seconds later, the speaker says he had sent the plans of his apartment.

Can we be sure?

Another American official in Vienna, Julius Goldschmidt, also had a wife named Ida. Grant fits the school news and the family’s habit of sending apartment plans, as well as the proposed reading of Julia. No surviving letter has yet been found that puts Grant at Wangemann’s phonograph.

About the audio restoration

The restoration starts with the National Park Service’s public 2011 transfer and reduces a wash of noise recurring roughly every 0.397 seconds. It uses no synthesized speech. The excerpt covers 20.8 to 31.0 seconds in both versions. The published transcription ends with the apartment plans. Most of the final minute is unclear.
