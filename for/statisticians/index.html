Statisticians and data scientists

modelling, estimating what is missing, image and text analysis

Many of these problems come down to estimating what is missing, separating signal from noise, or testing whether a pattern beats chance. Statisticians and data scientists can design those tests and make the reasoning explicit and repeatable.

13 problems need statisticians and data scientists

  1. 01
    How many people lived in the Americas in 1492?

    Combine the documentary table and archaeological proxies into regional estimates with uncertainty intervals, so the hemispheric total and its interval can be recomputed openly.

    Demography · Americas · 1492–1650also needs demographers, archaeologists and geneticists
  2. 02
    How many people lived in China around 1600, and how many died between 1620 and 1680?

    Flag outliers and internal contradictions in gazetteer household and land figures, and test each under-registration model against grain-price series and reclamation memorials.

    Demography · East Asia · 1580–1680also needs demographers and GIS specialists
  3. 03
    How did Gutenberg cast his type?

    Separate position-specific variation across B42 and DK-type copies from sort identity, and compare the observed shapes per letter with simulations of each casting process.

    Material & visual · Europe · 1450–1470also needs conservation scientists
  4. 04
    How many people were carried in the Indian Ocean slave trades, 1500–1850?

    Compute documented minimum counts by route, carrier and decade from the transfer records, quantify the gaps, and state an all-carrier total with an explicit uncertainty range.

    Economy · Indian Ocean · 1500–1850also needs archivists
  5. 05
    Does the Indus script encode language, or a non-linguistic sign system?

    Pre-register the classifiers and thresholds, then report error rates for the Indus corpus across alternative sign lists and segmentations.

    Languages & scripts · South Asia · 2600–1900 BCEalso needs archaeologists and computational linguists
  6. 06
    How many people were killed in the Irish rising of 1641?

    Cluster reports of the same killing across depositions into a deduplicated Armagh register, and compare eyewitness and hearsay totals against the 1650s examinations.

    Demography · Europe · 1641–1642also needs archivists and GIS specialists
  7. 07
    Do khipus record more than numbers, and can their non-numerical signs be read?

    Test whether proposed meanings predict unseen cords better than chance and better than the numerical baseline set by Medrano (2024).

    Languages & scripts · Americas · 1400–1800also needs archaeologists and computational linguists
  8. 08
    How many early printed editions have been lost without trace?

    Fit Chao1 and Efron–Thisted estimators by format, language and genre on a past snapshot of ISTC or GW, and check their predictions against editions found since.

    Texts & authorship · Europe · 1450–1600also needs archivists
  9. 09
    Were Mendel's pea-hybrid data too close to expectation, and if so, why?

    Encode every count in the 1866 paper and the Nägeli letters as one dataset, and test which documented procedures reproduce both Fisher's goodness-of-fit and 2:1 progeny-test anomalies.

    Science & knowledge · Europe · 1856–1866also needs geneticists
  10. 10
    Did Ptolemy observe the Almagest star catalogue or adapt Hipparchus's?

    Rebuild the competing tests on one edition of the catalogue matched to Hipparcos and Gaia DR3, reporting results by constellation, magnitude and source subset with doubtful identifications flagged.

    Science & knowledge · Mediterranean · 135 BCE–160 CEalso needs astronomers
  11. 11
    How literate was the population of late imperial China?

    Report the proportion of autograph signatures by decade, region, sex and role from a coded sample of several thousand contracts, and link scribes across documents.

    Texts & authorship · East Asia · 1644–1911also needs archivists
  12. 12
    How much American silver reached China between 1550 and 1800?

    Convert every published figure to tonnes of fine silver with documented assumptions, then propagate the uncertainty to show where mint, treasury and shipping series double count.

    Economy · Global · 1550–1800also needs economists and archivists
  13. 13
    Did Vermeer use a camera obscura or other optical aids?

    Test whether tracing from a projection, unaided camera observation or pin-and-string construction best fits the measured geometry across the paintings, and report the comparative fit.

    Material & visual · Europe · 1655–1675also needs conservation scientists
