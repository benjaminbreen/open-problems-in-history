Computational linguists

sign statistics, language models, stylometry

Undeciphered scripts and disputed texts can be approached through sign statistics, language models and stylometry. These problems need computational linguists who can set up tests that a proposed reading must pass.

7 problems need computational linguists

  1. 01
    When was Beowulf composed?

    Train a dating model only on independently dated Old English texts, treat copying as explicit noise, and test it on held-out texts preserved in late copies.

    Chronology · Europe · 680–1020also needs philologists
  2. 02
    Which lost works of Galen survive unrecognised in Arabic and Syriac?

    Model how Hunayn-school translators rendered Greek terms, using the Glossarium Graeco-Arabicum, and apply it to OpenITI texts to flag passages matching no extant Greek work.

    Texts & authorship · Middle East · 160–1270also needs archivists and philologists
  3. 03
    Does the Indus script encode language, or a non-linguistic sign system?

    Build comparison corpora of known scripts and non-linguistic systems trimmed to Indus text lengths, extending Sproat (2014), and test measures beyond entropy such as sign-position regularities.

    Languages & scripts · South Asia · 2600–1900 BCEalso needs archaeologists and data scientists
  4. 04
    Do khipus record more than numbers, and can their non-numerical signs be read?

    Using the Open Khipu Repository schema, write rival readings of colour, ply and attachment as models that predict non-numerical features of held-out cord groups.

    Languages & scripts · Americas · 1400–1800also needs archaeologists and data scientists
  5. 05
    What language does Linear A record, and can any proposed family beat chance?

    Write the Anatolian, Semitic and isolate hypotheses as explicit generative models and score them on held-out texts against shuffled sign values and unrelated control languages.

    Languages & scripts · Mediterranean · 1800–1450 BCEalso needs philologists
  6. 06
    Does Proto-Elamite record language, and is it the ancestor of Linear Elamite?

    Train sign-distribution models on proto-cuneiform, where logograms and phonetic signs are known, then test whether the residual Proto-Elamite signs contain a comparable phonetic subset.

    Languages & scripts · Middle East · 3300–1880 BCEalso needs philologists
  7. 07
    Was rongorongo an independent invention of writing?

    Compare rongorongo sign sequences against written languages and non-linguistic symbol systems, and test proposed readings by predicting held-out passages under alternative sign segmentations.

    Languages & scripts · Pacific · 1400–1870also needs archaeologists and conservation scientists
