Geneticists

ancient DNA of pathogens, people, plants and animals

Ancient DNA has already reshaped the history of plague and of human migration. These problems ask what sequencing pathogens, people, plants and animals could still settle, and where the samples are.

  1. How many people lived in the Americas in 1492?

    Assess whether ancient and modern genomic evidence on population size can serve as an independent check on regional estimates, and state its date resolution.

    Demography · Americas · 1492–1650
  2. Did the Black Death reach sub-Saharan Africa in the fourteenth century?

    Re-screen published African ancient-DNA shotgun data in the European Nucleotide Archive for Yersinia pestis, reporting coverage and detection limits for each individual.

    Disease · Africa · 1340–1500
  3. What caused the cocoliztli epidemics of 1545 and 1576 in New Spain?

    Screen securely dated 1545 and 1576 epidemic burials beyond Teposcolula-Yucundaa with methods able to recover RNA viruses and parasites as well as bacteria.

    Disease · Americas · 1545–1581
  4. How many people did the Justinianic Plague kill, and did it change late antique society?

    Align the pathogen genomes from Harbeck (2013), Wagner (2014) and Keller (2019) with dated burials, so each site with plague DNA enters the regional register with its date range.

    Disease · Mediterranean · 541–750
  5. Were Mendel's pea-hybrid data too close to expectation, and if so, why?

    Assess whether botanical factors in pea segregation, such as those Fairbanks and Schaalje (2007) tested experimentally, could shift Mendel's expected ratios enough to explain his close fits.

    Science & knowledge · Europe · 1856–1866
  6. Why did plague disappear from western Europe after the early eighteenth century?

    Sample burials from the last outbreaks in Provence, the Baltic, Messina, southern Italy and the Balkans to test local reservoir against repeated reintroduction from Asia.

    Disease · Europe · 1650–1850
  7. Where was the land of Punt?

    Build mitochondrial reference data for baboons in Eritrea, Sudan, Somalia and Yemen and compare it with New Kingdom baboon mummies such as British Museum EA 6736 and EA 6738.

    Places · Africa · 2500–1150 BCE
