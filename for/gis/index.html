GIS specialists

mapping historical places, routes and boundaries

Places named in historical sources often have to be located, mapped and linked to changing boundaries before anything else can be done. GIS specialists can build the spatial frameworks these problems depend on.

  1. Where was Agade, the capital of the Sargonic empire?

    Trace palaeochannels and mounds on CORONA and other declassified imagery, and map each dated textual constraint on distance and river crossing as a probability surface.

    Places · Middle East · 2350–539 BCE
  2. How many people lived in China around 1600, and how many died between 1620 and 1680?

    Key every gazetteer population, land and tax figure to CHGIS county boundaries by year, so densities and trends can be mapped across the core provinces.

    Demography · East Asia · 1580–1680
  3. How many people were killed in the Irish rising of 1641?

    Normalise place names against the Down Survey parish and barony maps so reported killings can be mapped and matched across depositions.

    Demography · Europe · 1641–1642
  4. Did the population of Ottoman Anatolia collapse in the late sixteenth and seventeenth centuries?

    Link villages across registers to modern coordinates through the gazetteer of Kabadayı et al. (2022), and map which settlements were abandoned rather than re-registered.

    Demography · Middle East · 1520–1700
  5. Where was Yamatai, the realm of Queen Himiko?

    Encode each reading of the Wei zhi itinerary, with its emended directions and distance units, and score the regions it predicts against the georeferenced site distribution.

    Places · East Asia · 180–280
