When did Europeans first describe the outbreak later called syphilis?

Europeans called it the French disease. A firsthand account from before Columbus returned in 1493 would change the chronology of Europe’s first recognized syphilis outbreak.

Why it matters

Ancient DNA has established that relatives of the bacteria that cause syphilis circulated in the Americas before 1492. In Europe, accounts often begin with Charles VIII’s army at Naples in 1495. A contemporary description from before Columbus returned in March 1493 would give the European chronology a different starting point.

Why it is open

A physician writing decades later could remember earlier patients in terms learned during the outbreak. Early words such as bubas could mean several kinds of sores. Even a firm printing date tells us only when a description was in circulation.

What would count as a solution

Establish the earliest independently dated outbreak accounts and what their writers actually witnessed. Give separate verdicts on reports placed before March 1493, between the return voyage and the Naples campaign, and after 1495. The texts can date recognition of an illness more securely than they can identify its bacterium.

Potential approach

  • Compare the earliest medical tracts with municipal orders and eyewitness chronicles. Record the event date, date of writing and date of the surviving copy separately.
  • Trace one author’s borrowing from another and check printed editions against surviving manuscripts.
  • Compare early American uses of bubas and Nahuatl disease terms in context, with specialists who can distinguish a translator’s choice of word from an observer’s description.
