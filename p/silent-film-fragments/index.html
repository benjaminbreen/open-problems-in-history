Which lost silent films survive in fragments whose titles are unknown?

A film archive may hold a few minutes of a movie whose title card is gone. A match with a contemporary description could put a lost performance back on screen.

Why it matters

preserves short pieces of film in its Bits & Pieces collection. Even when a full print has disappeared, a few identified scenes can show how an actor played a role or how a director staged an ending that survives elsewhere only as a synopsis.

Why it is open

A pilot matched fragment 23 to The Adorable Savage (1920) through its shipwreck, its fight and the arrival of an uncle. Eye had already published the identification in 2020. The example shows both how a copyright synopsis can recover a title and how an older catalogue description can make a known film look unidentified.

What would count as a solution

For each proposed title, show several distinctive details in the footage and in independently sourced period records. Check the archive’s attribution history and surviving-print records before counting a newly identified fragment or describing a film as lost.

Potential approach

  • Record intertitles, actions and props before searching for a title. Reserve a detail from the clip to test the best candidate.
  • Search copyright deposits and the Lantern trade press for the scenes in sequence. Check whether apparent corroboration comes from one publicity text printed twice.
  • Compare the candidate with rival films, allow for translated titles and different cuts, and give the holding archive the evidence and frame timings.
