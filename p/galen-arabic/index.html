Which lost works of Galen survive unrecognised in Arabic and Syriac?

Many Galenic works lost in Greek were translated into Syriac and Arabic in ninth-century Baghdad; some may survive in unedited manuscripts or in quotations not yet traced to their source.

Texts & authorship · Middle East · 160–1270digitization

Why it matters

Galen is the largest surviving author of Greek antiquity, yet his own lists and Hunayn ibn Ishaq's Risala name many works whose Greek text is lost, including logical, philosophical and Hippocratic commentaries. Several have already been recovered wholly or partly from Arabic (On Medical Experience, the later books of On Anatomical Procedures, , the commentaries on Epidemics II and VI), and each recovery has changed accounts of ancient empiricism, anatomy or Hippocratic exegesis. Identifying further texts would also clarify what Arabic medicine and philosophy actually inherited from Galen, as opposed to what later authors attributed to him.

Why it is open

Hunayn's Risala (c. 863, with later additions) describes about 129 Galenic works translated into Syriac or Arabic, but many of those translations survive only as quotations embedded in later compilations such as , Maimonides' Medical Aphorisms and 's biographical dictionary, often paraphrased and attributed loosely. Large numbers of Arabic medical manuscripts in Istanbul, Iran, the and elsewhere remain uncatalogued in detail or unedited, and the Syriac layer, as the shows, often survives only as erased undertext. Matching an Arabic paraphrase to a lost Greek work requires simultaneous command of Galen's Greek corpus, Hunayn's translation vocabulary and the Arabic compilers' citation habits, a combination few scholars have.

What would count as a solution

A passage or treatise in Arabic or Syriac is shown to derive from a Galenic work lost in Greek, by matching its title and content to Galen's or Hunayn's descriptions, by translation-vocabulary alignment with Hunayn's known versions, and by at least one independent witness (a second manuscript, a Greek or Latin fragment, or an attributed quotation elsewhere).

Potential approach

  • Build a register of every Galenic work lost or incomplete in Greek, starting from and Hunayn's Risala as edited by , with Galen's own descriptions of each work's contents.
  • Use research agents to align Galen's surviving Greek (Kühn's edition and the ) with Hunayn-school Arabic translations through the and 's lexical work, producing a model of how specific Greek terms and phrases were rendered.
  • Apply that model to machine-readable Arabic compilations in (, , Maimonides) to flag Galen-attributed passages that match no extant Greek work, and cluster them by likely source title.
  • Prioritise manuscripts that Sezgin, Ullmann and Fichtner list as unedited Galenic translations and seek imaging and catalogue collaboration with the , Iranian libraries and the where copies are not yet online.

Existing work

  1. 1925
    Gotthelf Bergsträsser, Hunain ibn Ishaq über die syrischen und arabischen Galen-Übersetzungen, Abhandlungen für die Kunde des Morgenlandes 17 (2). Leipzig: Brockhaus.First edition and German translation of Hunayn's Risala, the inventory of Galenic works translated into Syriac and Arabic.
  2. 1944
    Richard Walzer, Galen on Medical Experience: First Edition of the Arabic Version with English Translation and Notes, London: Oxford University Press for the Wellcome Historical Medical Museum.Edited from a single Istanbul manuscript a work on the empiricist–rationalist debate otherwise known only from small Greek fragments.
  3. 1969
    Malcolm Lyons (ed.), Galen: On the Parts of Medicine; On Cohesive Causes; On Regimen in Acute Diseases in Accordance with the Theories of Hippocrates, Corpus Medicorum Graecorum, Supplementum Orientale II. Berlin: Akademie-Verlag.Edited Arabic versions of works lost or incomplete in Greek, establishing the Supplementum Orientale series as the venue for such recoveries.
  4. 1970
    Manfred Ullmann, Die Medizin im Islam, Handbuch der Orientalistik I, Ergänzungsband VI.1. Leiden: Brill.Surveyed the Arabic Galen and listed known manuscripts of individual translations, many still unedited.
  5. 1997
    Gerhard Fichtner, Corpus Galenicum: Verzeichnis der galenischen und pseudogalenischen Schriften, Tübingen: Institut für Geschichte der Medizin; later editions updated online by the Berlin-Brandenburg Academy (CMG).Work-by-work bibliography of Galenic and pseudo-Galenic texts and their editions and translations, the starting inventory for tracing which survive in Arabic.
  6. 2005
    Véronique Boudon-Millot and Antoine Pietrobelli, De l'arabe au grec: un nouveau témoin du texte de Galien (le Vlatadon 14), Comptes rendus des séances de l'Académie des Inscriptions et Belles-Lettres 149 (2): 497–534.Announced the Thessaloniki manuscript that restored On Avoiding Distress in Greek and fuller Greek texts of works previously known partly through Arabic and Latin.
  7. 2013
    Siam Bhayro, Robert Hawley, Grigory Kessel and Peter E. Pormann, The Syriac Galen Palimpsest: Progress, Prospects and Problems, Journal of Semitic Studies 58 (1): 131–148.Reported the identification of Sergius of Resh'aina's sixth-century Syriac version of On Simple Drugs beneath a later liturgical text.
  8. 2016
    John C. Lamoreaux (ed. and trans.), Hunayn ibn Ishaq on His Galen Translations, Provo: Brigham Young University Press (Eastern Christian Texts).New edition and English translation of the Risala with its later additions; the link is to a review in the Journal of the American Oriental Society.
  9. 2016
    Uwe Vagelpohl (ed.), Galeni In Hippocratis Epidemiarum librum II commentariorum I–VI versio Arabica, Corpus Medicorum Graecorum, Supplementum Orientale V 2. Berlin: De Gruyter.Edited Hunayn's Arabic version of a commentary whose Greek original is lost.
  10. 2018
    Naima Afif, Siam Bhayro, Grigory Kessel, Peter E. Pormann, William I. Sellers and Natalia Smelova, The Syriac Galen Palimpsest: A Tale of Two Texts, Manuscript Studies 3 (1): 110–154.Described the imaging, codicology and recovered content of the palimpsest and its value for the Greek text.
  11. 2020
    Emilie Savage-Smith, Simon Swain and Geert Jan van Gelder (eds.), A Literary History of Medicine: The ʿUyūn al-anbāʾ fī ṭabaqāt al-aṭibbāʾ of Ibn Abī Uṣaybiʿah, Leiden: Brill (open access).Critical edition and translation of the thirteenth-century biographical dictionary whose Galen entry lists works and preserves quotations.
  12. 2020
    Naima Afif, Peter E. Pormann, William I. Sellers, Natalia Smelova and Siam Bhayro, The Syriac Text of Book Nine of On Simple Drugs: New Evidence from the Syriac Galen Palimpsest, Archives Internationales d'Histoire des Sciences 70 (184–185): 130–149.Used a lemmatised Greek–Syriac corpus with image analysis to reconstruct the Syriac and to propose readings for the Greek.
  13. 2022
    Uwe Vagelpohl (ed.), Galeni In Hippocratis Epidemiarum librum VI commentariorum I–VIII versio Arabica, Corpus Medicorum Graecorum, Supplementum Orientale. Berlin: De Gruyter.Edited the Arabic translation, the only complete witness to a commentary of which roughly a third is lost in Greek.
  14. 2024
    Peter E. Pormann, Translation into Syriac, Arabic, and Hebrew, in The Oxford Handbook of Galen (Oxford University Press): 512–534.Current survey of the Syriac, Arabic and Hebrew transmission and of works preserved only in translation.
  15. 2026
    Antoine Pietrobelli, New Considerations on the Manuscript Thessalonicensis Vlatadon 14, Religion in the Roman Empire 12 (2): 120–146.Reassessed the manuscript that yielded On Avoiding Distress, the main recent case of a lost Galenic work recovered in Greek.
