How did smallpox reach Aboriginal communities around Sydney in 1789?

Smallpox devastated Aboriginal communities around Sydney a year after the British arrived. Tracing its earlier appearances could clarify what part colonization played in those deaths.

Disease · Pacific · 1780–1790

Could use help from archivists and librarians and epidemiologists.

Did Europe’s three great medieval cattle plagues come from the east? →What caused the cocoliztli epidemics of 1545 and 1576 in New Spain? →

Why it matters

One explanation traces the epidemic to the British First Fleet at Sydney Cove. Another begins with Macassan trepangers, who sailed from present-day Indonesia to northern Australia to fish for sea cucumbers. The second explanation must also account for how the disease crossed the continent before April 1789.

Why it is open

A search of Dutch East India Company records found an outbreak at Makassar in 1782 and reports from Timor, Ambon and Banda later in the decade. It found no local outbreak in the Makassar papers searched for late 1788 and early 1789. Officials recorded some deaths among people enslaved by the Company, but their coverage of coastal communities was uneven.

What would count as a solution

An independently dated series of accounts linking an earlier outbreak to affected communities would strengthen one route. If the sequence breaks, an account of where each archive recorded illness, and whom it overlooked, would show how much the gap can tell us.

Potential approach

  • Check the Dutch transcriptions against the original pages and distinguish a report written in Makassar from a report about Makassar.
  • Compare the port chronology with surgeons’ journals, eyewitness accounts from Sydney Cove and the earliest inland reports.
  • Work with Aboriginal historians and custodians on the dates, places and permitted uses of oral accounts, including places omitted from colonial records.
