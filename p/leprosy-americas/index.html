What did Nahua writers say about the people Spaniards described as having leprosy?

Nahua writers in sixteenth-century Mexico described dancers playing someone with an illness a friar called leprosy, a clue to how people recognized and understood the condition.

Why it matters

In his 1571 dictionary, the friar Alonso translated the Nahuatl word teococoxqui as leproso, a person with leprosy. The same word appears in a Nahuatl account of a festival where dancers took on that role. What an audience recognized in the performance could tell us what it meant to live with the illness in central Mexico. Ancient DNA has already established that one cause of leprosy was present elsewhere in the Americas long before Europeans arrived.

Why it is open

A dictionary gives a translation in a few words. A describes someone acting a part. Neither tells us what a particular patient experienced. The Spanish and Nahuatl passages can also differ in how many conditions they describe. Even a detailed description would rarely identify which bacterium caused an illness.

What would count as a solution

Find independent accounts in which Nahua speakers describe a person, the signs of their illness, or how others treated them. Give the original words, a translation and the page image for each. Show where the descriptions agree and where a Spanish gloss groups different conditions together. A claim that a case occurred before the conquest needs a witness who actually dates it to that time.

Potential approach

  • Start with and the in Book 2 of the Florentine Codex. Read both columns of the festival passage and identify which words each writer used for the people the dancers portrayed.
  • Trace the term and its spelling variants into accounts of healing, daily life and local history. Compare a healer’s description, a friar’s biblical translation and a copied dictionary entry on their own terms.
  • Record who supplied each description, where they lived and when the account was written. Have a second Nahuatl reader check the passages where a different translation would change the conclusion.
