Did James Pawpaw’s remedy enter Sloane’s collection without his name?

A recipe credited to a Virginia healer shares a distinctive set of instructions with an anonymous manuscript in Hans Sloane’s collection.

Science & knowledge · Americas · 1729–1753digitization
Who supplied the medical knowledge that Hans Sloane's catalogues attribute to unnamed people? →

Why it matters

James Pawpaw, an enslaved Virginia practitioner, gained his freedom after disclosing medical knowledge. A secure attribution would trace some of that knowledge to a surviving object and show how it reached London.

Why it is open

The manuscript and the printed recipe share three plants and a sequence of preparations. The ingredients differ in places, and an annotation on the back might explain only a later addition.

What would count as a solution

A dated copy, letter or acquisition entry should link the manuscript to a named source or establish a shared intermediary for both recipes.

Potential approach

  • Transcribe both recipes in full and compare the sequence of operations. Check whether paper, handwriting and catalogue entries connect the manuscript to other Virginia material.
